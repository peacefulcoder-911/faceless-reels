In The Matrix, Neo's iconic moment isn't just seeing green code; it's a fundamental shift in perception. He stops seeing the world as solid objects and begins to perceive the underlying data stream. This is the Interface Illusion: our tendency to interact with a system's user interface as if it *is* the system itself, rather than a mere representation. We navigate a desktop, clicking 'files' and 'folders,' never thinking about the complex binary operations underneath. Neo initially saw physics as absolute, but once he understood the Matrix was merely an advanced interface, those 'laws' became programmable constraints. He didn't gain superpowers; he simply accessed the system's true control panel, bypassing the visual front-end. The real power wasn't in breaking the rules; it was in realizing there was only code, and the rules were just a GUI.
